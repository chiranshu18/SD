Ques. What is publisher subscriber model?
Ans.  The Pub-Sub model is a messaging pattern where publishers send messages (events) to a message broker, and subscribers receive those messages if they have expressed interest in them.
      - Publishers don’t know who the subscribers are — they just publish events.
      - Subscribers don’t care who the publisher is — they just consume relevant events.

      Simple Analogy 1
      - Think of YouTube:
      - Publisher = A YouTuber uploading a video.
      - Subscribers = People who subscribed to the channel.
      - Message Broker = YouTube platform, which delivers new videos to subscribers who opted in.

      SIMPLE ANALOGY 2
      - Think of a Magzine (Tech Today):
      - Publisher = The company that creates and prints "Tech Today" magazine. They produce new issues periodically.
      - Subscribers = You, the reader, who wants to receive "Tech Today" whenever a new issue comes out. You explicitly subscribe to this magazine.
      - Message Broker = A central entity (postal service) that handles the distribution. 

        How it works in the analogy:
        You (Subscriber) tell the magazine distributor (Broker) that you want to subscribe to "Tech Today."
        The "Tech Today" company (Publisher) finishes a new issue and send it to the magazine distributor (Broker).
        The magazine distributor (Broker) knows who subscribed to "Tech Today" and sends the new issue to all those subscribers.

        The publisher doesn't need to know who is reading their magazine, 
        and you, the subscriber, don't need to constantly check with the publisher for new issues. 
        There's a decoupling between the producer and consumer of information.

      Real-Life Example in System Design - PAYMENT SYSTEM:
      Publisher:
      - Order service publishes "Order Completed".
      Subscribers:
      - Inventory service reduces stock.
      - Notification service sends an email.
      - Analytics service logs the sale.

Ques. Why Is It Used / Needed?
Ans.  Various reasons:
      - Decoupling: Services don’t directly call each other → easier to maintain.
      - Scalability: Multiple subscribers can act independently.
      - Asynchronous Communication: Publishers don’t wait for subscribers to finish.
      - Fan-out Messaging: One event can trigger multiple actions.

Ques. Limitations / Drawbacks?
Ans.  Eventual Consistency - Subscribers process events at different times, data may not be immediately consistent.
      Complex Debugging -	Hard to trace which subscriber processed which event.
      Delivery Guarantees -	Ensuring "at-least-once" or "exactly-once" delivery is hard.
      Message Ordering - Hard to maintain strict order in distributed systems.

Ques. Real world examples?
Ans.  Uber --> When a ride ends → billing, driver payment, and trip history update happen via pub-sub.
      Netflix --> Publishes "new episode available" events → multiple services handle notifications, recommendations, etc.
      E-commerce (Amazon) -->	Order service publishes events → inventory, shipping, email services subscribe.
